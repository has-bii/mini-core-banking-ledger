# Still Bank — Logo Design Brief

## 1. Assignment

Design an original logo for **Still Bank** using the logo-design skill already invoked in this session. This is a standalone brief; all required brand context is below.

Create three tested logo concepts, present your recommendation, and **stop for the user's selection and explicit approval before producing the final logo kit**.

Scope: logo exploration, testing, and—after approval—production assets and concise usage guidance. Do not design UI screens, application components, frontend code, or a UI token system.

## 2. Brand

- **Exact name:** Still Bank.
- **Tagline:** none.
- **Product:** a personal mobile banking app for everyday customers, not business banking or an internal staff tool.
- **Personality:** Quiet Confidence—calm, precise, trustworthy, and approachable.
- **Desired impression:** everyday banking that feels reassuring and clear, without appearing cold, flashy, exclusive, or luxury-oriented.

Do not invent product capabilities, a demographic niche, regulatory status, or claims about security or financial outcomes. You may propose a creative interpretation of the name, but label it as a design rationale rather than an established business promise.

## 3. Creative direction

The identity should feel simple, distinctive, and carefully resolved. Aim for one clear idea per concept rather than illustrating a list of banking services.

- Explore different mark types rather than assuming a monogram or pictorial symbol is required.
- Across the three directions, explore at least two mark types. Each direction must demonstrate a readable Still Bank name treatment and an app-icon-capable identifier; explain any small-size adaptation.
- Use “Still” as a creative starting point, not a requirement to depict water or a pause icon.
- Investigate category conventions and look-alikes yourself. Treat familiar banking motifs as research findings to move beyond, rather than default solutions.
- Use references to learn construction and assess distinction, not to trace or imitate existing identities.

### Color

**Forest-green is the only prescribed color direction.** Propose the precise green and any supporting colors; no exact color codes or background colors are mandated.

Evaluate form in black first. The identity must also work in monochrome, reversed, and on light and dark backgrounds. Color must support a strong mark rather than compensate for a weak one.

## 4. Primary applications and success criteria

Lead with mobile-app branding:

- App icon.
- Favicon.
- Compact horizontal name lockup.
- Symbol-only and name-only use where appropriate.

A successful direction:

- Expresses the brand personality without relying on explanation.
- Keeps the name readable and the identifier recognizable at small sizes.
- Survives actual-size testing at 16, 24, 32, and 64 px; use a documented small-size cut when necessary.
- Works in one color and reversed, on light and dark backgrounds.
- Has balanced geometry, intentional spacing, and clean junctions.
- Is meaningfully distinct from relevant banking and fintech references.

Show identity applications or isolated mockups only; do not turn them into application-screen designs. Visual similarity research is not trademark clearance. State the limits of any checks performed.

## 5. Stage one — Concepts and approval checkpoint

Use the logo-design skill's research, exploration, SVG construction, testing, and refinement workflow. Apply this brief as the project context rather than repeating discovery questions it already answers. Ask only about genuinely blocking omissions; label non-blocking assumptions.

Deliver:

1. **Three distinct concepts**, each with an app-icon-capable identifier and a Still Bank name treatment.
2. **A rendered concept overview**, led by black/greyscale comparisons, showing the marks, compact lockups, and actual small-size samples.
3. **A short explanation per concept:** one-sentence idea, mark type, fit with the brand, and its principal risk or trade-off.
4. **Testing evidence:** rendered visual inspection, SVG checks, small-size tests, monochrome/reversed checks, and visual similarity comparisons. Report unresolved issues honestly.
5. **One recommendation**, justified against the brief rather than personal taste.

Render and inspect the artwork; source code alone is not a visual review. Keep meaningful iterations so they can be compared.

**Stop here.** Ask the user to select a direction or request changes, and offer the final kit. Do not choose on the user's behalf or produce the kit without explicit approval. If the user requests revisions, revise and repeat this checkpoint.

## 6. Stage two — Final kit, only after approval

For the approved direction, deliver:

- Refined SVG masters: symbol, wordmark, horizontal lockup, and stacked lockup.
- Approved color, black, white/reversed, and brand-monochrome variants.
- Small-size artwork where needed, plus app-icon and favicon exports.
- Transparent PNG exports at documented useful sizes, with appropriate solid-background icon exports where required.
- A compact presentation showing mobile-relevant identity applications without designing UI screens.
- Concise usage guidance: color specifications, clear space, minimum sizes, approved backgrounds, lockup relationships, typography/font licensing notes, and misuse examples.
- Handover notes listing the approved concept, file inventory, tests performed, and any unresolved limitations.

Finished logo SVGs must be self-contained vector artwork, with letterforms as paths rather than live text and no embedded raster artwork. Document the font source and license if typefaces are used during development.

Work in the repository-root `logo/` folder. Create it if it does not exist, and store all concept artwork, iterations, renders, tests, and final deliverables there. Keep concept explorations distinct from approved production assets. Report output paths. Do not modify this brief to imply approval.

## 7. Completion gates

### Concept stage complete

- [ ] Three genuinely different directions are rendered and presented.
- [ ] Each includes a readable name treatment and app-icon-capable identifier.
- [ ] Small-size, monochrome, reversed, and background tests are documented.
- [ ] Visual similarity research and its limitations are reported.
- [ ] A recommendation and trade-offs are stated.
- [ ] Work pauses for the user's selection and explicit kit approval.

### Final stage complete

- [ ] The user explicitly approved the selected direction and kit production.
- [ ] Required masters, variants, icon exports, and usage guidance are delivered.
- [ ] Final artwork is rendered, inspected, and checked after refinement.
- [ ] File inventory and outstanding limitations are recorded.
